Markov chain example: autoregressive process

Background and simulation

Markov chains are often defined by an algorithm that tells us how to obtain \(X_{n+1}\) from \(X_n\). The following example is known as “autoregressive process of order 1” (AR(1)):

Let \(Z_1, Z_2, \dots\) be independent, identically distributed continuous random variables with mean \(\lambda\), and \(\phi \in \mathbb{R}\) some constant. Then the series of random variables \(X_1, X_2\dots\) defined by the following recursion is an AR(1) process: \[ X_{n} = \phi X_{n-1} + Z_{n}. \] Certainly, the outcome depends on the starting value \(X_0=x_0\). In the following code, we assume normal distributed \(Z_i\sim N(\lambda, \tau^2)\). Experiment with what happens if you change \(x_0\), \(\phi\), \(\lambda\) and \(\tau\).
